# Labor Time Review Policy

## Purpose

This sample policy defines review guidance for warranty claims with labor times that exceed standard expectations.

This document is synthetic and intended only for development, testing, and demonstration of the Warranty Analyst Copilot knowledge base.

## Scope

This policy applies to warranty claims where:

- Submitted labor hours exceed standard labor allowance
- Additional diagnostic time is requested
- Multiple related labor operations are combined
- Complex repair conditions are claimed
- Repeated repairs increase total labor hours

## Standard Review Criteria

Warranty analysts should compare submitted labor hours against:

1. Standard labor operation allowance
2. Product configuration complexity
3. Diagnostic documentation
4. Supporting technician notes
5. Similar historical claims
6. Repair order detail level
7. Additional approved labor operations

## Supporting Documentation Requirements

Claims exceeding standard labor expectations should include:

- Technician explanation for additional time
- Diagnostic steps performed
- Confirmation of repair complexity
- Supporting measurements or test results when applicable
- Clear repair correction narrative

## Analyst Review Guidance

Additional review may be required when:

- Labor hours exceed standard allowance by more than 25%
- Diagnostic time is not explained
- The repair narrative is vague
- Similar claims typically require fewer labor hours
- Multiple overlapping labor operations are submitted
- Supporting evidence is incomplete

## Example Missing Information Flags

The following conditions may require follow-up:

- Missing explanation for additional labor time
- No documentation of extended diagnostics
- No justification for overlapping labor operations
- Repair narrative does not support claimed labor complexity
- No supporting technician notes

## Recommended Analyst Action

If labor hours exceed expected thresholds without sufficient documentation, the analyst should request clarification before final claim disposition.

The copilot should identify labor anomalies, summarize supporting evidence, compare against policy expectations, and recommend human review.
